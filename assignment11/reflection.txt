- With static visualizations, I think those are for more finalized versions or trying to tell
a specific story with the data without being swayed by other factors or analysis. With interactive 
visuals on the other hand, the user or myself can explore the data more and come up with a variety 
of stories and patterns. 

- Dashboards are especially helpful for real-time data exploration because they bring 
multiple views of the data into one place and let me interact with them quickly. 
Instead of rerunning code, I can adjust filters, date ranges, or categories on the fly and 
instantly see how the visuals change. That's helpful for monitoring live metrics or since I don’t 
have to build a new chart every time someone asks “what if we just look at X?”

- Interactive tools like Plotly can help with communication in professional settings since even non-technical indiviudals
can hover and look around the graph/visual on their own time. It also helps bridge the gap between technical and 
non-technical folks, because they don’t have to read raw tables or code, the only thing they need to do is just 
click around and understand the story in the data more intuitively.
